Week: 17
Task: Make an agent's writing stop sounding like an agent wrote it
Agent: Claude (Claude Code, terminal)

Prompt or workflow:
Anything an agent writes that a client will read carries a particular sound.
It is not incorrect, it is padded — it announces what it is about to say,
balances every clause against another, and recaps itself at the end. Readers
notice before they can name it, and once a client names it the work is
finished as a piece of writing.

"Make it sound more human" does not fix it. That produces the same prose with
contractions in it. What worked was refusing to accept the instruction in that
form:

  "don't tell me to make it natural. give me the specific phrases and
   constructions that give it away, as a list I can delete on sight"

That turns a taste problem into a checklist. The list that came back, shortened:

  - Throat-clearing openers: "In today's fast-paced world", "It's worth noting
    that", "Let's dive in".
  - The balanced-clause tic, which is the loudest single tell: "It's not just
    X, it's Y". One idea. State it. Move.
  - Empty intensifiers: seamlessly, robust, powerful, cutting-edge, leverage,
    unlock, elevate, delve.
  - Triads — three adjectives where one is true and provable.
  - Closing summaries. If the piece is short enough to follow, it needs no
    recap.
  - Hedge stacking: "may potentially", "could possibly".
  - Em-dash asides stacked three to a paragraph. One is fine.

Then the positive half, which is the part that actually changes the writing:

  - One idea per sentence.
  - Concrete over abstract. Not "improves efficiency" — "turns four days into
    eleven minutes". If you cannot name the number, say that you cannot.
  - Verbs, not nominalisations: "we verify every write", not "verification is
    performed on each write operation".
  - No claim without evidence in frame. If you say it is fast, the timer is on
    screen.
  - Vary sentence length deliberately. Three medium sentences in a row is a
    drone.

Result:
A skill that loads for anything a person will read or hear — narration,
captions, READMEs, release notes, commit bodies, client email. The prose it
produces is shorter and makes fewer claims, because the rule about evidence
deletes the sentences that were only there to sound confident.

Every prompt write-up on this site went through it, and so did this log.

What it saved: not time — it costs a pass. What it avoids is the specific
outcome where a client reads something and can tell. That is not recoverable by
editing afterwards.

The lesson for prompting: when you want a quality you can only recognise by
feel, ask for the list of things to delete rather than the quality itself. A
banned list is checkable. "Sound human" is not, and an agent asked for an
unmeasurable quality will produce a performance of it.
